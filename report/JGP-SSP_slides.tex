\section{The problem and the two questions}
\begin{frame}{Why this problem}
\textbf{Flexible manufacturing.} A CNC machine serves many part types from a
tool magazine of capacity $b$; every tool switch halts production while the
magazine is reconfigured --- switches are \emph{the} controllable cost
{\footnotesize[Crama et al.\ 1994]}.\\[2mm]
\textbf{Beyond machining.} The same structure --- a capacitated store serving
set-valued, reorderable requests --- appears in PCB assembly, warehouse
picking, and colour printing (ink cartridges as tools)
{\footnotesize[Burger et al.\ 2015]}.\\[2mm]
\textbf{Two live gaps in the literature.} The heuristic everyone uses has no
worst-case theory; the exact state of the art solves nothing at $n\ge30$.
This project attacks both --- and they meet at one number, $|\U|-b$.
\note{Open with the machine: magazine, switches, minutes lost per switch.
Generalise to the store-serving-requests pattern. Then name the two gaps ---
no guarantee for standard practice, no solver past n=30 --- and promise the
punchline: one quantity governs both.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The problem}
\textbf{Data.} Jobs $J$, tools $T$, magazine capacity $b$; job $j$ requires
$T_j\subseteq T$, $|T_j|\le b$. \\[1mm]
\textbf{Decision.} An order of the jobs and a loading policy; each tool
insertion costs $1$.\\[3mm]
\begin{itemize}
  \item Fixed order $\Rightarrow$ loading solved exactly by KTNS
        (keep tool needed soonest) in $O(n|T|)$ \hfill {\footnotesize [Tang--Denardo 1988]}
  \item Joint problem NP-hard \hfill {\footnotesize [Crama et al.\ 1994]}
\end{itemize}
\vspace{2mm}
\textbf{Practice: group, then sequence.} Partition jobs into fewest
magazine-fitting groups (JGP), order the groups (small TSP), run KTNS.
Applied verbatim in industry; assessed only \emph{empirically} until now
{\footnotesize [Burger et al.\ 2015]}.
\note{Define every object once, slowly. KTNS = optimal cache eviction: evict the
tool needed furthest in the future. JGP = job grouping problem = pack jobs into
fewest groups whose union fits the magazine. The literature measured the
decomposition's quality on instances, never its worst case. That gap in the
literature is our opening.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Two questions, one quantity}
\begin{block}{Q1 (heuristic)}
How far can group-then-sequence be from optimal? When is it exact?
\end{block}
\begin{block}{Q2 (exact methods)}
Can the SSP be solved through tool \emph{configurations} (column generation,
Benders), as the JGP so successfully is?
\end{block}
\vspace{2mm}
\begin{alertblock}{The organising quantity: the coverage bound $|\U|-b$}
Every used tool beyond the initial load must be inserted once, so
$\Zst\ge|\U|-b$. It is the LP value of every natural configuration relaxation
(proved) --- and on part of the instance space it \emph{is} the optimum.
\end{alertblock}
\note{Say explicitly: the same number answers both questions. Where |U|-b is
tight, the heuristic tends to be exact and exact methods finish at the root;
where it is loose, both must work. The benchmark later quantifies the split:
about half and half on the standard suites.}
\end{frame}

\section{A worst-case theory of the heuristic}
\begin{frame}{The gap identified exactly}
\begin{theorem}[Grouping exactness]
$\displaystyle \Zst=\min_{\mathcal P}\gamma(\mathcal P)$ over \emph{all}
feasible groupings. Hence
$\;\mathrm{gap}=H-\Zst=$ the price of restricting to minimum cardinality.
\end{theorem}
\vspace{1mm}
Proved zero-gap classes (every $b$): \quad
$\Kst\le2$, \quad $|\U|\le b+1$, \quad $\Zst\le2$.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The setup-cost spectrum: SSP $\longleftrightarrow$ JGP}
In practice a changeover costs a fixed setup $\rho$ beyond the per-tool swaps.
\begin{theorem}[Both ends explicit]
$\rho<\tfrac{1}{n-1}$ $\Rightarrow$ the augmented problem \emph{is} the SSP.\\
$\rho>H-\Zst$ $\Rightarrow$ every optimum uses minimum-cardinality grouping:
the heuristic is \emph{exactly optimal}.
\end{theorem}
\pause
\begin{itemize}
  \item the transition lives on $[\,1/(n-1),\ H-\Zst\,]$: it is governed by
        the \emph{gap itself};
  \item real tooling reports $\rho\approx3$--$60$ $\Rightarrow$ production
        sits far on the collapsed (JGP) side --- a rigorous licence for
        standard practice.
\end{itemize}
\note{Two messages. First, grouping is not an approximation device but an exact
lens; only the cardinality restriction costs. Second, the spectrum: both
thresholds explicit, transition governed by the gap itself; with realistic setup
costs the heuristic is provably optimal, answering the "real life" question of
how the SSP degenerates into the JGP.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The worst case, quantitatively}
For $\Kst=3$ (configurations $\subseteq\U$, $q=|\U|-b$, $R=\Zst-q$):
\[
  H_{\mathrm{walk}} \;=\; q+\min\ \min\bigl(x_{ab},x_{bc},x_{ca}\bigr),
  \qquad x_{uv}=\bigl|(C_u\cap C_v)\setminus C_w\bigr|
\]
\[
  \mathrm{gap}\;\le\;\max\Bigl(0,\ \min\bigl(q,\ \bigl\lfloor\tfrac{2b-q}{3}\bigr\rfloor\bigr)-R\Bigr)
  \qquad\text{(walk value; tight at every known walk extremum)}
\]
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Bounds for every $\Kst$}
\[
  \frac{H}{\Zst}\;\le\;\min(b,\ \Kst-1,\ q)
\]
\pause
\[
  \mathrm{gap}\;\le\;\Bigl\lfloor\frac{q\,(b(\Kst-1)-q)}{b+q}\Bigr\rfloor - R
  \qquad\text{(random ordering of the groups + Jensen)}
\]
\pause
Zero-gap classes, every $b$: \quad $\Kst\le2$, \quad $|\U|\le b+1$,
\quad $\Zst\le2$; \qquad and $\Zst\le3\Rightarrow\mathrm{gap}\le1$.
\note{The exact walk-cost formula: choose the middle configuration to shield
the overlap of the outer two -- padding arguments eliminate configurations
entirely. The counting bound comes from double counting tool degrees over
three configurations; the general-K* bound from a random ordering of the
groups plus Jensen. Tightness statements are for the walk value; see the next
frame for the walk-vs-KTNS separation.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Determining the worst case: a certificate, and a separation}
Natural candidate (all data $b\le4$): $\ \mathrm{gap}\le\Kst-2$. Two readings
of the heuristic: hold each group's configuration (\emph{walk} value
$H_{\mathrm{walk}}$), or re-evaluate the flattened order with KTNS
($H\le H_{\mathrm{walk}}$, the method as defined).
\pause
\begin{alertblock}{Certificate ($b=5$, $\Kst=3$, $|\U|=9$)}
$T=(\{0,1,2,4,7\},\{0,4,5\},\{1,5\},\{2,6,8\},\{3,4,5,8\})$:\quad
$\Zst=4,\ H_{\mathrm{walk}}=6$ --- the candidate \textbf{fails for the walk
model}, exactly on the boundary of the proved regimes, attaining our bound
with equality.
\end{alertblock}
\pause
\textbf{The separation:} on this very witness KTNS beats the walk: $H=5$,
true gap $=1$ --- and every known walk-extremal instance collapses the same
way. The candidate is \emph{reopened} for the heuristic itself; new:
$\Zst\le3\Rightarrow\mathrm{gap}\le1$ (every $b$, every $\Kst$).
\note{The honest arc: the regime theorems said where the candidate could
fail; the walk certificate was found exactly there; then the frame audit
showed the KTNS step beats the walk on precisely those instances. Upper
bounds all transfer (H <= H_walk). The open question is now the worst case of
the heuristic as actually run --- the walk theory is the proved scaffold.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The $4/3$ ratio, and what remains}
\begin{theorem}[$b=3$]
Every instance with $\Kst\le3$ satisfies $H/\Zst\le 4/3$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{itemize}
  \item ingredients: gap $\le1$ at $\Kst=3,b=3$ (counting law) and
        positive gap $\Rightarrow \Zst\ge3$ (small-optimum lemma);
  \item the case $\Kst\ge4$ is the \emph{one} surviving conjecture ---
        populated (witnesses with ratio $5/4$ exist), no violation known;
  \item the edge-family pattern ``positive gap $\Rightarrow\Kst=3$'' does
        \emph{not} extend --- found and corrected this month.
\end{itemize}
\note{Honest status: 4/3 is now a theorem on most of its range and exactly one
regime remains conjectural. Mention the corrected assumption openly -- an
earlier draft leaned on the edge-family pattern; mixed-size instances broke it;
the proof route was repaired to not need it.}
\end{frame}

\section{Which structure decides the gap?}
\begin{frame}{Which structure decides these questions?}
\textbf{Negative (gap side).} Two instances with the \emph{same} circuit
clutter, same $\Kst$, same $\Zst$ have different gaps ($0$ vs $1$)
$\Rightarrow$ no job-compatibility invariant can decide exactness;
tool-level overlaps are essential.
\vspace{2mm}
\begin{theorem}[Covering side: odd rings, via blocking duality]
Clusters over maximal groups; blocker $=$ minimal covers ($\Kst=$ its min
size). On the $k$-ring, $b=3$: fractional cover $=k/2$, $\Kst=\lceil
k/2\rceil$ --- \textbf{tight iff $k$ even}.
\end{theorem}
Odd circular structure: first provable obstruction for the set-covering
relaxation behind JGP branch-and-price --- the parallel of odd holes.
\note{For the polyhedral session: blocker = the dual clutter; the pair here is clusters
versus covers; Lehman transfers idealness between them. The hand computation:
weight one-half per adjacent pair. Question for the session: is odd-ring-like
structure the ONLY obstruction (an idealness characterisation)?}
\end{frame}

\section{Two exact solvers}
\begin{frame}{Solver 1: branch-and-Benders-cut}
\textbf{Why Benders fits.} Fixed order $\Rightarrow$ loading is polynomial
(KTNS). Put the \emph{order} in an integer master, settle the \emph{loading}
exactly in a subproblem:
\[
  \min\ \theta \quad\text{s.t.}\quad \text{Ham.\ path via depot},\quad
  \theta\ge\textstyle\sum_{ij} w_{ij}x_{ij},\quad \theta\ge|\U|,\quad
  \text{cuts}
\]
\begin{itemize}
  \item loading subproblem = totally unimodular LP $=$ KTNS $\Rightarrow$
        \emph{exact} Benders optimality cuts from its dual;
  \item plus combinatorial cuts and triplet bounds; subtours separated
        exactly at integer candidates; $2^3$ cut configurations ablated;
  \item the coverage row $\theta\ge|\U|$ is our theory's one-line fix ---
        the re-run measures exactly its effect.
\end{itemize}
\note{The textbook precondition: hard ordering wrapped around an easy inner
problem. Master = TSP skeleton with a cost variable theta; every candidate
order is priced exactly by the KTNS LP dual, so cuts are never heuristic.
Walk through one cut generation if asked: order fixed, dual solved,
coefficients are linear in the assignment variables.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Solver 2: position formulations and branch-and-price}
\textbf{The obstacle.} The natural configuration model needs exponentially
many subtour constraints; MTZ repairs are exact only with one potential per
configuration --- exponential again (proved).\\[1mm]
\textbf{The route: fix a polynomial row set, $O(n|T|)$, generate columns.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{PCF$'$}: column $=$ configuration at a position; rows: one
        configuration per position, cover every job, count insertions.
        LP value $=|\U|-b$ (proved) --- the diagnostic instrument;
  \item \textbf{PTF}: column $=$ \emph{transition} $(C\to C')$ per step, with
        tool-by-tool flow consistency; LP $\ge|\U|-b$ and \emph{strictly
        above} on witnesses --- first in the family to beat the bound;
  \item MILP pricers; robust branching on aggregated $a_{t,p}$ keeps the
        pricing problem structurally intact.
\end{itemize}
\note{PCF-prime inherits the coverage ceiling -- that is a theorem, so its
role is measurement. PTF pays a heavier pricer for a strictly stronger bound:
71 of its 72 solves close in a single node. If Ryan--Foster comes up: RF
exists for masters where variable branching destroys the pricer; here the
aggregate a_{t,p} branches robustly, so RF solves a problem we do not have.}
\end{frame}

\section{The computational study}
\begin{frame}{The computational instrument}
\textbf{Why run it.} The theorems single out $|\U|-b$; the benchmark measures
it across the standard suites. And a prior question: the SSP's polynomial
loading subproblem is the textbook precondition for Benders --- untested until
now.
\vspace{1mm}
\begin{itemize}
  \item methods: 8 Benders configs (ours), LSS '04, Catanzaro-F4 '15,
        SSPMF '24, our branch-and-price (PCF$'$, PTF)
  \item 1{,}421 standard instances ($n\le40$, $|T|\le60$); 3600\,s / 600\,s;
        pinned threads; early stop after 8 straight failures
  \item one canonical metric: every returned sequence re-scored by KTNS
  \item integrity: $806$ instances solved by someone --- \textbf{zero}
        cross-method disagreements
  \item code, baselines, and per-instance results:
        {\scriptsize\url{https://github.com/shankar-n/JGP-SSP-Codes-Shankar}}
\end{itemize}
\note{Stress the protocol choices: re-running baselines ourselves on one
machine/solver generation (cross-paper wall-clocks are meaningless); one
KTNS-based metric so objective conventions cannot distort; early-stop so weak
configurations do not burn weeks.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Experimental design}
\textbf{Environment.} LIMOS SLURM cluster, homogeneous AMD EPYC 7452 nodes;
CPLEX 22.1.1, 4 dedicated threads per run (B\&P single-threaded, PySCIPOpt);
16\,GB/run; time limits 3600\,s (primary) / 600\,s (secondary); all other
solver parameters at defaults.\\[2mm]
\textbf{Protocol.} Instances ordered easiest-first by $(n,m,b,\text{density})$,
identically for every method; a configuration is retired after 8 consecutive
non-optimal runs. Attempted counts are therefore \emph{outcomes}; results are
reported over uniform suite denominators.\\[2mm]
\textbf{Validation.} All exact solvers pre-checked against brute force on
small instances; every returned sequence re-scored with KTNS in a single cost
convention; 806 instances solved by $\ge$1 method, \textbf{zero}
cross-method disagreements.
\note{One minute here buys credibility for everything after: same machine
pool, same solver generation, same metric, same ordering. Early stop saves
budget on failing arms; the uniform denominators in the next slide count
unattempted as unsolved, so nobody is flattered.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The diagnosis: bound-limited, mechanically}
Pre-fix: optimum found in seconds, \emph{proof} bound frozen for the full hour
($\sim$250k nodes, $\sim$75k cuts); fractional cuts \textbf{structurally
inactive}. \textbf{With the coverage row} $\theta\ge|\U|$: $1/9\to43/73$
(primary), $26/48\to244/318$ (secondary); 122/287 solves at the root node,
median $0.05$\,s; the ablation flattens; \emph{all} 104 remaining timeouts
still hold the known optimum --- nothing outside the bound-tight class is
solved.
\vspace{2mm}
\begin{block}{The measured split}
Among the $806$ solved instances: $\ 48.0\%$ have $\Zst=|\U|-b$ exactly
(upper-biased estimate). The suites split in half between the regime where
heuristic and exact methods win at the root, and the regime where new
mathematics is required.
\end{block}
PCF$'$/PTF confirm the same law from the branch-and-price side
($15\%$ of PCF$'$ optima at the root; PTF's stronger bound is the one lever
that exceeds $|\U|-b$).
\note{The frozen-bound mechanism: the master carries only pairwise information,
which vanishes at loose capacities -- our coverage row was the one-line fix,
and the completed re-run confirms it: the tight half is recovered entirely,
the loose half untouched -- the bound, not the search, is the frontier. The 48/52 split is
the report's thesis made empirical.}
\end{frame}

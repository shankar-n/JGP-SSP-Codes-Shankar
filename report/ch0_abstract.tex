\begin{abstract}
\setstretch{1.0}
\noindent
The Job Sequencing and Tool Switching Problem (SSP) asks for an order of jobs and a
feasible sequence of tool magazines that minimise tool insertions. Loading is
polynomially solvable once the order is fixed, but choosing the order is NP-hard. A
common practical alternative first partitions the jobs into the fewest
magazine-feasible groups and then sequences those groups.

This report gives an instance-sensitive account of that two-phase method. It separates
the cost of a fixed-configuration walk through the groups from the cost obtained after
the final job order is reloaded optimally by Keep Tool Needed Soonest (KTNS). The SSP
optimum is proved to equal the cheapest configuration walk over all feasible groupings;
the walk gap is therefore exactly the price of restricting the first phase to a
minimum-cardinality grouping. The resulting theory gives zero-gap classes and
instance-dependent upper bounds. For the KTNS-postprocessed method itself, a connected
family with capacity six is constructed for every $g\ge1$ with
\[
  \lvert\U\rvert=8g+1,\qquad \Kst=3g,\qquad
  \Zst=8g-5,\qquad H=9g-5.
\]
Hence its additive loss is $g$ and is unbounded even on connected instances. The exact
worst-case ratio remains open.

On the exact side, the report develops a branch-and-Benders-cut solver whose subproblem
optimises loading exactly, and two position-indexed configuration formulations. The
position--configuration relaxation is proved to equal the coverage bound
$q=\lvert\U\rvert-b$. The position--transition relaxation is at least $q$, but exceeds
it on only two of the $232$ recorded canonical roots.

The completed campaign contains $1{,}410$ canonical instances, twelve tested
configurations, and all $16{,}920$ planned outcomes under a common one-hour protocol.
Using the fixed planned denominator,
the reimplemented compact baselines SSPMF, Catanzaro F4, and LSS solve respectively
$1{,}028$, $876$, and $833$ instances; the strongest tested Benders setting solves
$724$. These figures compare the implementations in this repository, with different
effective thread settings, and are not a literature-wide state-of-the-art ranking.
For Benders, the coverage row equals the recorded root value on
$9{,}067/10{,}392$ runs ($87.2\%$), and coverage tightness is the strongest measured
separator of closure: the plain solver closes $94\%$ of known-optimum tight cases and
$40\%$ of loose ones. Fractional separation generates about $67$ million cuts and
reduces the search tree, but in the paired records improves no terminal bound and
closes fewer instances; the comparison is confounded by its required presolve change.
The evidence therefore diagnoses a certificate bottleneck in the tested Benders
implementation and motivates stronger global bounds. The contribution is a corrected
structural theory and a reproducible algorithmic diagnosis, not a faster solver claim.
\end{abstract}

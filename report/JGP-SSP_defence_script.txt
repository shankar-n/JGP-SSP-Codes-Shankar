SLIDE 1 -- TITLE
Target: 0:35

Good morning. This internship studies two linked problems: grouping jobs that can
share one magazine, and sequencing all jobs so that tools are inserted as few times
as possible. The main question is whether an exact loading oracle makes Benders
decomposition effective for the sequencing problem. My answer is structural: the
solver often finds the schedule, but struggles to certify the reinsertion cost that
occurs across several positions.

Transition: I will begin with the machine model, then prove the connected grouping
gap, and finally use that same reinsertion lens to read the exact campaign.


SLIDE 2 -- THE SSP MACHINE AND PROBLEM
Target: 1:05

Each job requires a subset of tools. The machine magazine holds at most b tools, and
all tools required by a job must be present while that job is processed. If the next
job requires a missing tool, that tool must be inserted and, when the magazine is
full, another tool must be removed.

We choose a permutation of the jobs and a feasible magazine state at every position.
The Tool Switching Problem, or SSP, minimises the total number of insertions. In the
small example on the slide, neighbouring jobs share tools, so those tools can remain
loaded. A different order can evict a tool and later require it again. That return is
a reinsertion, and it is the central quantity in both halves of this defence.

Transition: The important asymmetry is that loading and ordering have very different
complexities.


SLIDE 3 -- FIXED-ORDER LOADING IS EXACT; ORDERING IS NP-HARD
Target: 1:00

Once the job order is fixed, Keep Tool Needed Soonest, or KTNS, produces an optimal
loading in O of n times the number of used tools. At every eviction it removes an
unrequired tool whose next use is furthest in the future. Tang and Denardo proved this
fixed-order result.

Choosing the order is the hard part. Crama and co-authors proved the SSP NP-hard for
every fixed capacity b at least two. This separation suggests decomposition: let a
master choose the order, solve loading exactly for that order, and send information
from the exact loading problem back to the master.

Transition: Before presenting that solver, I will locate it in the exact-method
literature and explain why lower bounds are the recurring issue.


SLIDE 4 -- EXACT-METHOD LITERATURE LINEAGE
Target: 1:10

The exact literature is a sequence of attempts to strengthen the ordering bound.
Laporte and co-authors showed that the original Tang--Denardo relaxation can have
value zero on every instance, and introduced the LSS formulation. Catanzaro and
co-authors later developed five compact formulations; their Formulation 5 is stronger
than Formulation 4 and is their recommended model.

The multicommodity SSPMF formulation proves that its relaxation is exactly the tool
count minus capacity when every declared tool is used. In my notation that is the
coverage bound q. Later work includes the strengthened JGSMF model of Akhundov and
Ostrowski, and the dynamic-programming and A-star methods of Legrand and co-authors.

The campaign here reimplements LSS, Catanzaro Formulation 4, and SSPMF. These are
reimplemented reference baselines, not the current state of the art. JGSMF,
Catanzaro Formulation 5, and the latest combinatorial exact algorithms are not
performance baselines in this study.

Transition: A parallel literature avoids solving the SSP directly by grouping first.


SLIDE 5 -- GROUP, SEQUENCE, THEN APPLY KTNS
Target: 1:10

A group is feasible when the union of the tools required by its jobs fits in one
magazine. The Job Grouping Problem partitions the jobs into the minimum number K star
of feasible groups.

The standard pipeline has three steps. First, solve the grouping problem. Second,
choose an order of the resulting group configurations, usually as a small travelling-
salesman problem. Third, flatten the groups into one job sequence and apply KTNS to
that sequence.

This is not only a theoretical construction. Burger and co-authors use it for an
industrial printing problem. Crama and van de Klundert proved a classical b-
approximation for the construction. What was missing was an instance-sensitive theory
for the value after the final KTNS step: exact cases, finite witnesses, and the actual
gap caused by insisting on the fewest groups.

Transition: That theory starts by separating unavoidable coverage from genuine
reinsertions.


SLIDE 6 -- COVERAGE, Z STAR, AND THE HEURISTIC ENVELOPE H
Target: 1:15

Let U be the set of tools required by at least one job, and use the free-initial cost
convention. At most b tools can be present in the free first fill. Therefore q,
defined as the cardinality of U minus b, counts tools that must each be inserted at
least once.

Every feasible full-magazine schedule therefore has the exact identity

cost equals q plus R,

where R counts reinsertions of tools that appeared earlier, were removed, and later
returned. Hence the SSP optimum is Z star equals q plus R opt.

For the grouping pipeline, H is the minimum KTNS cost over every minimum-cardinality
grouping, every group order, and every order of jobs inside each group. H is an
optimistic envelope, not the output of a particular tie-breaking implementation. It
always satisfies H at least Z star. A positive gap therefore isolates a restriction of
the pipeline itself rather than a poor implementation choice.

Transition: The smallest positive actual gap has only four jobs.


SLIDE 7 -- THE FOUR-JOB SEED
Target: 1:05

Take capacity four and the jobs

A equals {1,2}, B equals {3,4}, C equals {1,3,5,6}, and D equals {2,5,6,7}.

The only feasible pair is A with B. Therefore every minimum grouping consists of the
group {A,B} and the two singleton groups C and D, so A and B must be consecutive in
every sequence admitted by the grouping pipeline.

The unrestricted order B,C,A,D attains the coverage bound q equals three, so Z star
equals three. Exhausting the six grouped orders up to reversal gives best KTNS cost
four. Thus K star equals three and H equals four. One forced adjacency creates one
avoidable reinsertion.

Transition: I now amplify this one-unit seed while preventing disconnected copies from
being a loophole.


SLIDE 8 -- BUILDING THE CONNECTED FAMILY
Target: 1:05

For every positive integer g, take g private copies of the seven original tools and
the four seed jobs. Add one universal tool u to every job. For each copy i, also add a
private marker tool v sub i to all four jobs in that copy. Finally, raise capacity from
four to six.

The universal tool makes the construction connected: every pair of jobs intersects
through u, and the bipartite job--tool incidence graph is also connected. The private
marker identifies each copy and prevents small jobs from different copies from being
packed together at capacity six.

Transition: The first proof obligation is to determine the minimum grouping exactly.


SLIDE 9 -- WHY K STAR EQUALS 3g
Target: 1:10

Each large job C sub i or D sub i now requires its four original tools, the marker v
sub i, and the universal tool u. It fills all six slots, so it cannot share a group
with any other job.

Within copy i, the two small jobs A sub i and B sub i together require four original
tools, v sub i, and u, exactly six tools, so they form a feasible pair. Two small jobs
from different copies require their four private original tools, two different marker
tools, and u: seven tools. They cannot share a group.

Therefore each copy contributes exactly the three groups {A sub i,B sub i}, {C sub
i}, and {D sub i}. The minimum grouping is forced, and K star equals 3g.

Transition: The unrestricted SSP optimum nevertheless reaches coverage exactly.


SLIDE 10 -- WHY Z STAR EQUALS 8g MINUS 5
Target: 1:10

There are seven original tools and one marker for each copy, plus the universal tool.
Thus the total number of used tools is 8g plus one. With capacity six, the coverage
bound is q equals 8g minus five.

Process the copies consecutively. Inside copy i use the seed order B sub i, C sub i,
A sub i, D sub i. Keep the universal tool loaded throughout. The marker occupies one
slot, and the four remaining non-universal slots reproduce the capacity-four loading
of the seed order. On entering a new copy, every new private tool is appearing for the
first time; no private tool ever has to return.

This constructs a schedule with no reinsertion, so it attains q. Therefore Z star
equals 8g minus five.

Transition: Minimum grouping destroys that zero-reinsertion order once in every copy.


SLIDE 11 -- WHY H EQUALS 9g MINUS 5
Target: 1:20

Every minimum-group sequence makes A sub i and B sub i consecutive. At any job of copy
i, the required universal tool and marker leave at most four slots for that copy's
seven original tools. If those original tools had no reinsertion, restricting the
schedule to this copy would give a capacity-four, zero-reinsertion seed order with A
and B consecutive. The exact seed table proves that impossible.

So every copy contributes at least one private reinsertion. Counting first appearances
and these g returns gives H at least q plus g, which is 9g minus five. Processing the
copies consecutively in a best grouped seed order attains exactly one reinsertion per
copy, so equality holds.

The theorem is therefore

Z star equals 8g minus five, H equals 9g minus five, and H minus Z star equals g.

The additive gap is unbounded even on connected instances. The ratio tends to nine
eighths, not infinity, so this does not contradict the classical multiplicative
b-approximation.

Transition: The theorem identifies the missing cost. I now ask whether the exact
Benders master represents the same object.


SLIDE 12 -- THE BENDERS LOOP AND THE CONVENTION SHIFT
Target: 1:05

The Benders master chooses a job order and uses one continuous variable theta as an
optimistic loading cost. For a candidate order, the loading subproblem is solved
exactly. If theta is too low, a valid optimality cut is returned to the master. The
process runs inside branch and bound until the lower bound meets the incumbent.

The implementation uses the empty-start convention, so the first magazine fill is
charged. Under the preprocessing assumptions, empty-start cost equals free-initial
cost plus b. Consequently the free-initial coverage bound q corresponds to the master
row theta at least the cardinality of U. Every cross-method result is re-evaluated with
the same empty-start KTNS oracle; native objectives are never compared directly.

Transition: Here is the exact master on which those cuts act.


SLIDE 13 -- THE EXACT MASTER PROBLEM
Target: 1:15

The master has a depot d, binary arc variables x sub i j, and theta. One incoming and
one outgoing arc at every node, together with subtour-elimination constraints, encode
a Hamiltonian path through the jobs.

Before any Benders cut, theta has three kinds of lower bound. The pairwise row charges
the minimum switch forced by each selected adjacent pair and charges the first job on
the depot arc. The coverage row is theta at least the cardinality of U. Optional
triplet rows use unions of three consecutive jobs.

Subtour constraints are separated lazily at integer candidate solutions. This is exact
for integer feasibility. Fractional subtour-elimination constraints were not separated
or tested in this campaign. Root-bound statements therefore describe the implemented
degree master with its stated rows and Benders user cuts, not a master strengthened by
the full fractional subtour closure.

Transition: For a valid order, the subproblem supplies an exact cost and an arc-
supported dual cut.


SLIDE 14 -- THE EXACT LOADING ORACLE AND ITS DUAL CUT
Target: 1:20

For each job and tool, y records whether the tool is in the magazine, and z records an
insertion. Requirement rows force required tools present. Capacity rows limit every
magazine to b tools. Transition rows make z pay when a selected arc enters a state in
which a tool is newly present, and depot rows charge the initial load.

For a fixed Hamiltonian path, this linear program has the consecutive-ones structure
and is integral. Its optimum is exactly the KTNS cost of the order. The dual feasible
region is independent of the candidate order, so any dual-feasible point gives an
affine Benders cut in the arc variables that is valid for every order and tight at the
current order when the dual is optimal.

The depot coefficients are essential. An early implementation omitted them and could
cut off valid optima; 193 random-instance violations exposed the defect. The term was
restored before the reported campaign.

Transition: The campaign varies how and where these valid cuts are generated.


SLIDE 15 -- THE NINE BBC REGIMES
Target: 1:15

There are nine Benders regimes. BBC-LP uses dual optimality cuts at integer candidates.
BBC-K uses the cheaper KTNS combinatorial cut. BBC-LP plus F and BBC-K plus F also
separate dual Benders cuts at fractional relaxation points. BBC-LP plus T adds triplet
bounds. The three additions on the fractional base are C for the conflict-graph row,
H for the hybrid-genetic start, and P for Pareto cut lifting. BBC-LP plus ACC combines
C, H, and P.

Two qualifications are essential. Every plus-F regime also disables CPLEX presolve so
that the user-cut callback remains valid. A plus-F comparison is therefore a regime
comparison, not a clean causal estimate of fractional cuts alone. Also, plus H supplies
both a MIP start and a seeded Benders cut. Its roughly two-second heuristic
preprocessing and seed-cut construction occur before the recorded CPLEX timer.

Transition: With those regime definitions fixed, I can state exactly what the campaign
does and does not compare.


SLIDE 16 -- COMPLETE PROTOCOL AND VALIDATION
Target: 1:20

The primary campaign contains 1,410 canonical instances and 12 configurations, giving
16,920 planned canonical method--instance rows. Every row is present after recovery;
there are zero missing and zero error rows. Every run has a one-hour CPLEX limit and
16 GiB, and no outcome-dependent early stopping is used.

The cluster allocated four cores to each run, but the implementations did not use them
equally. The Benders solver explicitly ran with one CPLEX thread. The three compact
baselines ran with four CPLEX threads. Therefore this is not a thread-balanced speed
comparison, although all methods retain the same planned denominator and nominal
resource envelope.

At least one method proves an optimum on 1,107 instances. On the 988 instances proved
by at least two methods, all 41,514 pairwise comparisons agree after common KTNS re-
evaluation. Small exact tests and an independent verifier provide additional checks;
they do not constitute a proof that the implementations are defect-free.

Transition: The first result is simply how many optima each reimplemented method
certifies.


SLIDE 17 -- UNDER THIS PROTOCOL, COMPACT BASELINES CLOSE MORE
Target: 1:00

The three reimplemented compact baselines certify the most instances: SSPMF closes
1,028, Catanzaro F4 closes 876, and LSS closes 833. The best Benders regime is BBC-LP
with 724, followed by BBC-LP plus T with 723 and BBC-K with 722. The remaining Benders

Thus the decomposition is not a faster solver in this campaign. These counts compare

methods. The thread asymmetry also means that runtime rankings must be read with

Transition: The aggregate ranking is less informative than the structural split by
whether reinsertion is required.


SLIDE 18 -- CLOSURE IS STRONGLY ASSOCIATED WITH COVERAGE TIGHTNESS
Target: 1:10

Among the 1,107 known optima, 520 are coverage-tight and 587 are coverage-loose.
BBC-LP certifies 490 of the tight cases, or 94.2 percent, but only 234 of the loose
cases, or 39.9 percent. That is a 54-point association with whether the optimum needs
at least one reinsertion.

The compact controls prevent the stronger interpretation that loose instances are
intrinsically hard. SSPMF closes all 520 tight cases and 508 loose cases. Catanzaro F4
closes 369 tight and 507 loose. LSS closes 369 tight and 464 loose.

Therefore coverage tightness is a strong predictor of closure for BBC-LP in this
campaign. This is association, not causation, and it is not a universal hardness label
for the instance.

Transition: Incumbent and root records show why the association points toward
certification rather than schedule discovery.


SLIDE 19 -- THE SOLVER OFTEN HAS THE SCHEDULE BUT NOT THE CERTIFICATE
Target: 1:10

BBC-LP certifies 402 instances within one second and 724 within one hour. Among its 383
time-limited runs for which an optimum is known from another proof, 223 already hold an
incumbent of exactly that value. That is 58 percent of the measured unfinished cases.
Across all 680 time-limited BBC-LP runs, however, the median terminal optimality gap is
23.1 percent.

The root evidence is consistent with the same diagnosis. Across the nine Benders
regimes, 9,067 of 10,392 recorded canonical roots equal the coverage row exactly. That
is 87.2 percent. This does not prove that coverage causes every failure, but it shows
that the implemented master usually begins with no bound stronger than coverage.

Transition: The ablation tests whether the attempted search and bound modifications
change that outcome.


SLIDE 20 -- THE IMPLEMENTED FRACTIONAL REGIME CUT NODES, NOT TERMINAL BOUNDS
Target: 1:15

The fractional regime BBC-LP plus F certifies 655 instances. Adding the hybrid-
genetic regime gives 686, a gain of 31. Adding the conflict row gives 654, and Pareto
lifting gives 618. BBC-LP, which removes fractional separation and restores presolve,
certifies 724, a difference of 69.

The fractional regime generates 67,047,595 Benders user cuts and reduces median nodes
from 77,439 to 3,701. Yet among 1,404 paired terminal bounds, the plus-F bound is higher
zero times, equal 1,226 times, and lower 178 times.

These numbers do not identify the causal effect of fractional cuts, because plus F
also disables presolve. Likewise, the plus-H gain cannot be assigned only to the
incumbent: that regime includes a MIP start, a seed cut, and roughly two seconds of
preprocessing outside the recorded CPLEX timer. The defensible result is narrower:
none of the tested bound-oriented regimes improves the fixed-budget solve count over
BBC-LP.

Transition: I can now separate the proved contribution, the measured diagnosis, and
the next research step.


SLIDE 21 -- THE EVIDENCE POINTS TO REINSERTION ACROSS A SPAN
Target: 1:15

On the structural side, the cost identity says that everything above coverage is
reinsertion. The four-job seed is smallest, and the connected family proves an
unbounded additive gap for the best minimum-group-and-KTNS envelope. Its ratio tends to
nine eighths.

On the exact side, coverage tightness is strongly associated with BBC-LP closure, many
unfinished runs already contain an optimal schedule, and the tested local bound regimes
do not improve the number of proofs. Because the experiment has thread, presolve, and
timer asymmetries, this is a diagnosis of the implemented regimes, not a causal theorem
and not a state-of-the-art solver claim.

The lower-risk publication route is the audited grouping and KTNS theory. The higher-
risk exact route is a configuration-consistent model that enforces one whole-magazine
path across positions. Its success criterion is explicit: raise loose-instance roots
materially above q and convert that gain into more certified optima under a genuinely
matched protocol.

Closing line: The missing object is not another local copy of coverage. It is a
reinsertion across a span.


BACKUP SLIDE 22 -- CONNECTED PROOF LEDGER

Use this slide if asked for the lower-bound count in the connected theorem.

For copy i, let x sub i be the number of its seven original tools placed in the free
initial magazine. Let y be the number of initially loaded tools among the universal
tool and the g markers. Because the initial magazine has capacity six, the sum of the
x sub i plus y is at most six.

The seed argument forces one reinsertion in every copy. Copy i therefore needs at least
seven minus x sub i first appearances plus one reinsertion, which is eight minus x sub
i later insertions. The universal and marker tools need at least g plus one minus y
insertions. Summing gives

sum over i of eight minus x sub i, plus g plus one minus y,
at least 9g minus five.

The consecutive grouped construction attains the same value, so H equals 9g minus
five. Connectivity follows because every job contains u. The theorem concerns an
unbounded additive gap; H divided by Z star tends to nine eighths.

BACKUP SLIDE 24 -- WINDOW REDUNDANCY AND PCF/PTF

Use this slide for questions about whether a span-based inequality or a configuration
formulation already fixes the bound.

The proposed integer window statement is valid: every tool used somewhere in a window
must either be present at the window start or be inserted inside it. But its robust
fractional encoding is implied by the existing transition rows tool by tool. Summing
those implied inequalities gives the implemented window row. It cannot strengthen the
LP, and the regression experiment confirms identical roots on all 37 paired cases.

The independent position--configuration relaxation PCF prime equals q on all 2,108
runs with a recorded root. The position--transition formulation PTF exceeds q on only
two of 232 recorded roots, and only by one unit. These results do not prove that every
configuration model is limited to coverage. They show that per-tool or per-position
fractional consistency still permits mixing. A credible next model must enforce one
capacity-feasible path through complete magazine configurations, and it must be judged
by both root improvement and additional fixed-budget proofs.

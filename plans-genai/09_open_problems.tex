\section{Gap and Approximation Ratio (Part V)}
\begin{op}
\textbf{OP\,1 (Tight ratio for $b=3$; conjecture).} Is $H/\ZSSP \le 4/3$ for every
$b=3$ instance, with equality only at the $6$-job ring?
\emph{Status:} open. Strong evidence --- no counterexample in the $10691$ exhaustively
enumerated $b=3$ edge instances, nor in a further $6065$ instances with size-$3$ jobs, nor
in the copy-paste or ring families (Part~V; $\approx 17$k instances total). The earlier
route via $\sum_k R_k \le H/4$ is \emph{false} (counterexample
$\{a,b,c\},\{a,b,d\},\{c,d,e\}$) and has been retracted.
\end{op}
\begin{op}
\textbf{OP\,2 (Extremal ratio for general $b$).} What is $\sup_I H/\ZSSP$ as a function
of $b$? \emph{Status:} partially resolved. Exact witnesses give ratios $4/3$ ($b=3$), $4/3$ ($b=4$),
and $3/2$ ($b=5$). These are \emph{proved} lower bounds on $\sup_I H/\ZSSP$ (the instances and
their $Z^*,H$ are computed exactly), so it is a \textbf{theorem} that $\sup_I H/\ZSSP>4/3$ for
$b\ge5$ --- i.e.\ the $4/3$ bound is specific to $b=3$, \emph{not} universal. What is \emph{not}
proved (three data points only): monotonicity in $b$, the growth law (does it $\to2$? is it
unbounded?), and the $b=3$ upper bound itself (OP\,1).
\end{op}
\begin{op}
\textbf{OP\,3 (Structural characterisation of zero gap).} Give a necessary-and-sufficient
structural condition for $H=\ZSSP$. \emph{Status:} open, partly clarified. By OP\,4 the gap
vanishes for \emph{every} instance once grouping choice is unrestricted, so the question is
really: when does some \emph{minimum-cardinality} ($\Kstar$-)grouping already attain
$\ZSSP$? Two sufficient conditions are proved (the heuristic meets either lower bound
$\Kstar-1$ or $|U|-b$; Part~V), and empirically every positive-gap $b=3$ edge instance has
$\Kstar=3$ with the ring/prism boundary structure. A clean necessary-and-sufficient
characterisation (e.g.\ via a forbidden ``ring obstruction'') is missing.
\end{op}
\section{Grouping Selection (Parts V--VI)}
\begin{op}
\textbf{OP\,4 (Joint grouping--sequencing).} \emph{Structural part now resolved} (Part~V,
grouping-exactness theorem): $\ZSSP=\min_{\mathcal{G}}\mathrm{GTSP}(\mathcal{G})$ over
\emph{all} feasible groupings, so the JGP$+$GSP gap is \emph{exactly} the price of
restricting to minimum-cardinality groupings (proved; verified on $1306$ instances). In
particular a sub-optimal grouping ($K>\Kstar$) can strictly beat the heuristic (the
$6$-ring $K=4$ grouping attains $\ZSSP=3<4=H$). \emph{What remains open} is the
\textbf{algorithmic} problem: efficiently optimise over the (exponentially many) feasible
groupings --- a polynomial algorithm, or NP-hardness, for this joint grouping-and-sequencing
objective.
\end{op}
\begin{op}
\textbf{OP\,5 (Beyond one-directional lookahead).} The MWHP ordering result (Part~VI) is
exact only for the one-directional lookahead policy and coincides with the optimum at zero
slack; with positive slack, bidirectional padding can be strictly cheaper (the
sliding-window family has true cost $|U|-b=(K{-}1)(r{-}\mathrm{ov})-(b{-}r)$, below the MWHP
value $(K{-}1)(r{-}\mathrm{ov})$). A polynomial exact GSP for positive-slack groupings, or a
proof of NP-hardness, is open.
\end{op}
\section{Exact Formulations (Parts II, IV)}
\begin{op}
\textbf{OP\,6 (Exchange argument / cluster-MTZ exactness; central to Part~IV).} \textbf{RESOLVED (negative).} The cluster-aggregated MTZ formulation is \emph{not} exact. Its constraint is representable for a configuration path \emph{iff} every ordered cluster pair is traversed at most once \emph{and} the induced precedence digraph (edge $j'\!\to\!j''$ whenever an arc runs from a $j'$-config to a $j''$-config) is \emph{acyclic}. The acyclicity fails under \textbf{job domination}: if $T_i\subseteq T_j$, every config covering $j$ also covers $i$, so an arc between two such configs forces both $j\!\to\!i$ and $i\!\to\!j$, a $2$-cycle. Explicit counterexample ($b=3$): jobs $\{1,4\},\{1,3,4\},\{1\},\{1,2,4\}$ have $\ZSSP=1$ but cluster-MTZ optimum $2$ (brute-force verified; $19$ counterexamples in $\approx7.9$k instances). \textbf{For column generation use per-configuration MTZ or lazy SEC/PCTSP cuts (both exact), not cluster-MTZ.} The job-indexed MTZ of the BBC master (Part~VIII) is unaffected.
\end{op}
\begin{op}
\textbf{OP\,7 (LP strength and net solver benefit of MTZ).} The cluster-MTZ relaxation is no
tighter than SEC and is weakened further by aggregation (Part~IV). Quantify the LP gap on SSP
instances (with and without Desrochers--Laporte lifting), and measure whether removing row
generation yields a net branch-and-price speed-up versus the SEC and SSPMF formulations on the
Catanzaro Tabela1C set. \emph{Status:} open, computational.
\end{op}
\begin{op}
\textbf{OP\,8 (Fractional Benders cuts).} The branch-and-Benders solver (Part~VIII,
\texttt{08\_branch\_and\_benders.tex}) currently adds optimality cuts only at integer master
solutions (KTNS subproblem). Deriving valid cuts at \emph{fractional} master solutions from a
relaxation of the KTNS recursion (min-cost-flow / interval-matrix lower bound, or the
totally-unimodular tooling LP) --- enabling user cuts that tighten the bound before branching
--- is open. Cut \emph{lifting} (certifying a cut for all sequences sharing a $0$-block
structure) is the related practical lever.
\end{op}
\section{Cost-Function Variants (Part VII)}
\begin{op}
\textbf{OP\,9 (Tight collapse condition).} The conditions (C1)+(C2)+(C3) are sufficient for
collapse of an SSP variant to the JGP; the necessity analysis (Part~VII) shows (C3) necessary
and (C1),(C2) dispensable. A single necessary-and-sufficient characterisation of collapse,
beyond the reduction to the threshold $\rho_c(I)$, is open.
\end{op}
\begin{op}
\textbf{OP\,10 (Setup-time regime in practice).} For the magazine/tool setup-time ratio
$\rho$, the TD-SSP approximation ratio $R(\rho)$ decreases from $\le 4/3$ at $\rho=0$ to $1$
as $\rho\to\infty$, with ring threshold $\rho_c=1$ (Part~VII). Whether the manufacturing
regime $\rho\in[3,60]$ (Privault--Finke) makes JGP+GSP exactly optimal on broader instance
classes (not just ring-like) is open.
\end{op}
